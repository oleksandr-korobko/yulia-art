My practice explores the interplay between material presence and historical memory, investigating how objects, spaces, and archives can embody and transmit experiences of displacement, conflict, and resilience. Working across installation, sculpture, ceramics, and material-based painting, I engage with found objects, archival materials, and legal documents to examine how personal and collective histories intersect with broader socio-political realities.

Central to my work is the concept of material witness—the idea that objects and substances carry traces of lived experience. Whether through the fragility of ceramic forms, the tactile imprint of earth in paintings, or the spatial dynamics of installation, I seek to create environments that invite contemplation and personal reflection. My installations often function as immersive spaces where visitors encounter the weight of history through physical presence rather than narrative explanation.

The ongoing conflict in Ukraine deeply informs my practice, as I navigate questions of belonging, loss, and the possibility of reconstruction. By working with materials that embody both vulnerability and endurance, I explore how art can serve as a space for processing trauma, preserving memory, and imagining pathways toward healing and justice.
