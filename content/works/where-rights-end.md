Where Rights End is a floor-based installation in which the Universal Declaration of Human Rights is printed and fixed directly onto the gallery floor using adhesive spray, then encircled by yellow-black warning tape. Positioned underfoot, the document is subjected to physical proximity, repeated crossing, and potential damage, transforming a foundational legal text into a vulnerable material surface.

Rather than functioning as a symbol of protection, the declaration here becomes a site of exposure. Language is placed under pressure – not through overt destruction, but through routine contact and collective indifference. The work reflects on how legal guarantees collapse not only through violent rupture, but through normalisation, passivity, and the gradual erosion of responsibility.

Where Rights End exposes the fragility of social systems and the failure of international commitments once assumed to secure peace and safety. The warning tape marks a boundary that is simultaneously visible and ineffective, raising questions about who respects such limits, who crosses them, and under what conditions ethical responsibility is suspended. The installation confronts viewers with the unsettling proximity between declared values and lived reality, asking how long rights can exist as language once their protection is no longer enacted.
